# Project description \- [“ShortFind” UI/UX Uni group project](https://www.figma.com/design/1aviQ8BkMinrMPUDFVm7Be/HCI-group-4?m=auto&t=fx99tgPwTYqu1UfQ-1)

---

## Context:

CS200 Human-Computer Interaction Coursework, Spring 2026, Team of 4

## Tools

Google Forms, Google docs, Figma, Bluetooth speaker, Cross DJ app.

## Task

Design an analogue, everyday object and augment it with a unique Internet-of-Things capability — something that doesn't already exist in this form on the market. The brief required grounding the design in real user research (not just a hypothetical need), building and testing a working prototype through Wizard-of-Oz methods, and evaluating it against both expert heuristics and real users before submitting a final report.

## Process

**Autoethnography** We each logged our own daily frustrations for a week, then compared notes as a group. The clearest, most universal pattern across all four of us was losing personal items — phones, keys, medication — so we built the project around solving that.

**Initial design ideas → picking an idea** We landed on a smart sticker: a slim Bluetooth-enabled tag that could be stuck onto an object or slipped inside one (like a bag), making almost anything trackable rather than being limited to specific "smart" devices.

**Personas** We built three personas — an elderly user with memory decline, a working parent managing multiple misplaced items, and a painter working in messy/outdoor conditions — and used each one to stress-test the design. This directly drove three concrete design changes: a docking station with alert feedback (so the scanner itself doesn't get lost), unique musical themes per sticker (so users can tell items apart), and a protective film layer for durability.

**Submitted Video 1** Our first design used a physical scanner device. Our tutor's feedback on this submission was that the scanner was pulling focus away from the sticker — our actual "analogue object" — so we cut it.

**Refinement of idea \+ sketching** We replaced the scanner with a phone app, and reworked the audio feedback logic: volume indicated distance, and musical clarity (via a mid-frequency filter) indicated how accurately the user was facing the item.

**Prototyping** We built a Wizard-of-Oz prototype: a Figma UI with interactive user flows, paired with live-mixed audio via the Cross DJ app and a Bluetooth speaker, so a facilitator could simulate the tracking response in real time as a test participant moved around a room.

**Video 2** Demonstrated two use-case scenarios — locating a single item within a room, and locating two items spread across a house — showing the full interaction loop from opening the app to homing in on the object.

**10 heuristics of usability** We ran a Nielsen heuristic evaluation on the prototype. It scored well on “match with the real world” and “minimalism”, but flagged gaps in “system status feedback”, “navigation consistency”, and “error recovery” — which shaped what we tested for next.

**Usability study with 5 real users** Participants (ages 18–59) worked through scenario-based tasks with the Wizard-of-Oz prototype while thinking aloud, then completed a questionnaire on ease of use, audio effectiveness, and perceived usefulness.

**Final evaluation → submitted report** Combined the heuristic evaluation and study data into a full write-up of findings and next steps.

## Result

From the usability study, we found that the prototype was rated on average 4.4/5 for ease of understanding and 4.2/5 for how well the audio guidance helped users locate items, with an average task completion time of 108 seconds and full willingness among participants to use the system again.

Planned refinements: stronger visual design and colour scheme, clearer icons/symbols to reduce reliance on text labels, more precise audio feedback, and a visual aid to support users in noisy environments or in case of a defective speaker.

## Reflections

The hardest part was picking up the workload after one of our teammates stopped responding to messages out of nowhere \- we had to coordinate as a team to redistribute his share of the work. On the design side, writing the personas was the toughest part \- completely unfamiliar territory for me going in. The most fun part was making the videos, as it felt like we were making short films and it was a great way to show progress in the project. This was a genuinely fun assignment to work on, and I'm looking at ways to take “ShortFind” further, potentially as a basis for my final year dissertation.
